\section{The intermediate language}
\label{sec:dsl}

We give the language a semantics by translation into quantifier-free linear
arithmetic over the reals and integers ($\mathrm{QF\_LIRA}$), which is decidable
and for which the verification questions of Section~\ref{sec:verdict} are
discharged by an SMT solver. The translation is the implementation: there is no
second, informal reading of a rule.

\subsection{Signature}

A \emph{registry} is a signature
\[
  \Sigma \;=\; (V,\ \tau,\ \mu,\ \rho,\ E,\ \beta)
\]
where $V$ is a finite set of variable names and $E$ a finite set of event names,
and
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
  \tau : V \to \{\mathsf{real},\mathsf{int},\mathsf{bool},\mathsf{code},\mathsf{date}\}
    & \text{type} \\
  \mu : V \rightharpoonup \mathcal{U}
    & \text{unit (partial)} \\
  \rho : V \to \{\mathsf{obs},\mathsf{act}\}
    & \text{observation or action} \\
  \beta : V \cup E \rightharpoonup \mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{R}
    & \text{terminology binding (partial)}
\end{array}
\]
$\beta$ maps a name to a code system, a code, and a \emph{release version}.
Where $\beta$ is undefined the name is \emph{unbound}: a predicate the source
states but does not define, carried deliberately rather than discarded
(Section~\ref{sec:verdict}). Write $\mathrm{Num} = \{\mathsf{real},\mathsf{int}\}$.

$\Sigma$ is \emph{closed}: it is generated by the terminology-binding stage, and
the constrained decoder that produces rules is given $V$ and $E$ as literal
enumerations. A concept that failed to bind is therefore not merely discouraged
but absent from the output space of the generator.

\paragraph{Derived signature.}
Temporal and relative-change operators are interpreted over symbols induced by
$\Sigma$ rather than written by the generator, so that each such symbol has
exactly one definition:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
  \mathsf{occ}_e : \mathsf{bool}, \quad \mathsf{dse}_e : \mathsf{int}
    & \text{for each } e \in E \\
  v@e : \mathsf{real}
    & \text{for each } e \in E,\ v \in V \text{ with } \tau(v) \in \mathrm{Num}
\end{array}
\]
$\mathsf{occ}_e$ records that $e$ occurred, $\mathsf{dse}_e$ the days since it
did, and $v@e$ the value of $v$ at the time of $e$. Write $\Sigma^{+}$ for
$\Sigma$ extended with these.

\subsection{Syntax}

\[
\begin{array}{rcll}
  \varphi & ::= & \mathsf{Cmp}(v, \oplus, k)
    & v \in V,\ \oplus \in \{<,\le,>,\ge,=,\neq\} \\
  & \mid & \mathsf{Btw}(v, l, h, \iota_l, \iota_h)
    & l,h \in \mathbb{Q},\ \iota_l,\iota_h \in \mathbb{B} \\
  & \mid & \mathsf{Since}(e, n, \iota)
    & e \in E,\ n \in \mathbb{N} \\
  & \mid & \mathsf{Delta}(v, e, d, p, q)
    & d \in \{\downarrow,\uparrow\},\ p \in (0,1),\ q \in (0,1) \cup \{\bot\} \\
  & \mid & \neg\varphi \;\mid\; \textstyle\bigwedge \Phi \;\mid\; \bigvee \Phi
    & \Phi \text{ a finite non-empty sequence}
\end{array}
\]

Four atoms and three connectives. Disjunction is primitive: in a preliminary
study, an expression language without it forced an \textsc{and}-for-\textsc{or}
encoding that inverted the referral verdict on $69.5\%$ of the patient space.

\subsection{Well-formedness}

Judgement $\Sigma \vdash \varphi$. Each failed premise corresponds to a
diagnostic code emitted by the validator, given in brackets.

\[
\frac{v \in V \quad \tau(v)=\mathsf{bool} \quad k \in \mathbb{B} \quad \oplus \in \{=,\neq\}}
     {\Sigma \vdash \mathsf{Cmp}(v,\oplus,k)}
\qquad
\frac{v \in V \quad \tau(v)\in\mathrm{Num} \quad k \in \mathbb{Q}}
     {\Sigma \vdash \mathsf{Cmp}(v,\oplus,k)}
\]
\smallskip
\[
\frac{\tau(v)\in\mathrm{Num} \quad l<h}
     {\Sigma \vdash \mathsf{Btw}(v,l,h,\iota_l,\iota_h)}
\;\textsc{[empty\_interval]}
\qquad
\frac{e \in E \quad \beta(e)\ \text{defined} \quad n \ge 0}
     {\Sigma \vdash \mathsf{Since}(e,n,\iota)}
\;\textsc{[unbound\_event]}
\]
\smallskip
\[
\frac{\tau(v)\in\mathrm{Num} \quad e \in E \quad 0<p<1 \quad
      (q=\bot \ \vee\ p \le q<1) \quad v \nsim e}
     {\Sigma \vdash \mathsf{Delta}(v,e,d,p,q)}
\;\textsc{[circular\_baseline]}
\]

The side condition $v \nsim e$ requires the measured variable and the baseline
event to denote distinct concepts. Without it, a rule may measure a quantity
against itself --- ``the dose is at most half the dose at the time the drug was
being taken'' --- which fixes no point in time and is satisfied by every
assignment. It is well-typed, non-vacuous by the syntactic test, and compiles;
only this condition excludes it.

A $\mathsf{Since}$ window requires a \emph{bound} event because it lowers to a
retrieval against a code, whereas a $\mathsf{Delta}$ baseline lowers to a
declared null (Section~\ref{sec:verdict}) and may therefore anchor to an unbound
event.

\subsection{Interpretations}

An interpretation $I$ of $\Sigma^{+}$ is a \emph{partial} assignment of the
symbols of $\Sigma^{+}$ to values of their sorts. A patient is such an $I$;
the variables are not indexed by patient, because no guideline statement relates
two patients, so a formula over a population decomposes into independent
per-patient formulas. A satisfying assignment returned by the solver
\emph{is} a patient.

Partiality is the normal case, not an edge case: unbound names have no data
source, and a bound name may simply be unrecorded.

\subsection{Semantics}

$\llbracket\cdot\rrbracket$ maps a well-formed expression to a
$\mathrm{QF\_LIRA}$ formula over $\Sigma^{+}$. Write $\le_\iota$ for $\le$ when
$\iota$ holds and $<$ otherwise.

\[
\begin{array}{rcl}
  \llbracket \mathsf{Cmp}(v,\oplus,k) \rrbracket
    &=& v \oplus k \\[2pt]
  \llbracket \mathsf{Btw}(v,l,h,\iota_l,\iota_h) \rrbracket
    &=& (l \le_{\iota_l} v) \wedge (v \le_{\iota_h} h) \\[2pt]
  \llbracket \mathsf{Since}(e,n,\iota) \rrbracket
    &=& \mathsf{occ}_e \wedge (\mathsf{dse}_e \le_\iota n) \\[2pt]
  \llbracket \mathsf{Delta}(v,e,\downarrow,p,q) \rrbracket
    &=& \bigl(v \le (1-p)\cdot v@e\bigr)
        \;\wedge\; \bigl[q \neq \bot \Rightarrow v \ge (1-q)\cdot v@e\bigr] \\[2pt]
  \llbracket \mathsf{Delta}(v,e,\uparrow,p,q) \rrbracket
    &=& \bigl(v \ge (1+p)\cdot v@e\bigr)
        \;\wedge\; \bigl[q \neq \bot \Rightarrow v \le (1+q)\cdot v@e\bigr] \\[2pt]
  \llbracket \neg\varphi \rrbracket &=& \neg \llbracket \varphi \rrbracket
  \qquad
  \llbracket \textstyle\bigwedge\Phi \rrbracket = \bigwedge_{\psi\in\Phi} \llbracket \psi \rrbracket
  \qquad
  \llbracket \textstyle\bigvee\Phi \rrbracket = \bigvee_{\psi\in\Phi} \llbracket \psi \rrbracket
\end{array}
\]

The conjunct $\mathsf{occ}_e$ in $\mathsf{Since}$ is load-bearing. Omitting it
lets a window be satisfied by a patient for whom the event never occurred, and
``the test was never done'' is the commonest real violation of a monitoring
recommendation.

\paragraph{Time.} $\mathsf{Since}$ is a bounded past-time metric operator,
flattened to derived state at a single time point. The flattening is contained
(the symbols $\mathsf{occ}_e, \mathsf{dse}_e$ are produced by this translation,
never by the generator) and visible (the fragment classifier of
Section~\ref{sec:fragments} still reports $\mathrm{MTL}$). Genuinely multi-visit
predicates --- ``on two occasions at least 90 days apart'' --- are outside the
language and are refused rather than approximated.

\subsection{Rules and deontic force}

A \emph{rule} is $R = (\mathit{id},\ \varphi_{\mathrm{when}},\
\varphi_{\mathrm{then}},\ \delta,\ \pi)$ with
$\delta \in \{\mathsf{Obl},\mathsf{Per}\}$ and $\pi$ provenance (source span, page).
Its denotation is the material implication
\[
  \Phi_R \;=\; \llbracket \varphi_{\mathrm{when}} \rrbracket
               \;\rightarrow\; \llbracket \varphi_{\mathrm{then}} \rrbracket .
\]
$\delta$ is a conditional deontic operator: $\mathsf{Obl}(\mathrm{then}\mid
\mathrm{when})$ for an obligation, $\mathsf{Per}(\mathrm{then}\mid\mathrm{when})$
for a permission. We use the dyadic form and never nest deontic operators, which
avoids the paradoxes of monadic standard deontic logic. Prohibition is not a
third case: $\mathsf{F}(p) = \mathsf{Obl}(\neg p)$, written with a negated
consequent. Human-readable fields (an action verb, a target gloss, a GRADE
strength) accompany a rule for review but carry no semantics.

The \emph{violation condition} is
\[
  \mathrm{viol}(R) \;=\;
  \begin{cases}
    \llbracket \varphi_{\mathrm{when}} \rrbracket \wedge
      \neg \llbracket \varphi_{\mathrm{then}} \rrbracket
      & \delta = \mathsf{Obl} \\
    \bot & \delta = \mathsf{Per}
  \end{cases}
\]
so that no patient, and no completion of a partial $I$, yields a violation of a
permission. The guarantee is structural rather than a condition applied to a
verdict after the fact.

\subsection{Domain theory}

$\mathcal{D}_\Sigma$ is a finite conjunction of constraints asserted with every
query:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
  a \le v \le b & \text{for bound } v \text{ with } \tau(v)\in\mathrm{Num}
                  \text{ and a curated plausible range } [a,b] \\
  \bigvee_{c \in \mathrm{codes}(v)} v = c & \text{for } \tau(v)=\mathsf{code} \\
  0 \le \mathsf{dse}_e \le H & \text{for each } e \in E \\
  v@e > 0 \ \wedge\ a \le v@e \le b & \text{for each induced baseline}
\end{array}
\]
Plausible ranges are curated rather than derived: terminologies do not publish
physiological bounds. Without them the solver returns counterexamples no
clinician accepts. The positivity of $v@e$ is required: a zero baseline makes
every value simultaneously a $50\%$ reduction of it, so the obligation becomes
unfalsifiable. \emph{Unbound names are deliberately unconstrained} --- an axiom
about a hole would be a claim the source does not make.

\subsection{Verdicts}
\label{sec:verdict}

Let $P$ be the conjunction of equalities pinning the values a patient record
supplies. Unsupplied symbols remain free, so $\mathcal{D}_\Sigma \wedge P$
denotes the set of \emph{completions} of the record.

\begin{definition}[Verdict]
For a rule $R$ with $\delta = \mathsf{Obl}$,
\[
\mathrm{verdict}(R,P) =
\begin{cases}
  \textsc{must-violate}
    & \text{if } \mathcal{D}_\Sigma \wedge P \wedge \Phi_R \text{ is unsat} \\
  \textsc{certified-compliant}
    & \text{if } \mathcal{D}_\Sigma \wedge P \wedge \neg\Phi_R \text{ is unsat} \\
  \textsc{may-violate} & \text{otherwise.}
\end{cases}
\]
\end{definition}

The verdict is thus a modal statement over completions: \textsc{must-violate}
means \emph{no} completion complies, \textsc{certified-compliant} that
\emph{no} completion violates, and \textsc{may-violate} that the record does
not determine the question.

\begin{proposition}
If $\mathcal{D}_\Sigma \wedge P$ is satisfiable, the three cases are mutually
exclusive and exhaustive.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Exhaustive by construction. If the first two both held, then
$\mathcal{D}_\Sigma \wedge P$ would entail both $\neg\Phi_R$ and $\Phi_R$,
contradicting its satisfiability.
\end{proof}

A two-valued checker must collapse \textsc{may-violate} into one of the others,
and either choice is wrong in a way the user cannot see: reporting compliance
hides a missing datum, and reporting violation manufactures an alert from
absent data.

\paragraph{Permissions.} A permission admits no violation, so compliance is the
wrong question; the informative one is applicability. With
$A = \llbracket\varphi_{\mathrm{when}}\rrbracket$ and
$T = \llbracket\varphi_{\mathrm{then}}\rrbracket$, and writing
$\mathrm{sat}(\chi)$ for satisfiability of $\mathcal{D}_\Sigma \wedge P \wedge \chi$:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
  \neg\mathrm{sat}(A) & \textsc{option-not-applicable} \\
  \mathrm{sat}(A) \wedge \mathrm{sat}(\neg A) & \textsc{option-available} \\
  \neg\mathrm{sat}(\neg A) \wedge \neg\mathrm{sat}(\neg T) & \textsc{option-taken} \\
  \neg\mathrm{sat}(\neg A) \wedge \neg\mathrm{sat}(T) & \textsc{option-not-taken}
\end{array}
\]
None of the four is an accusation.

\subsection{Non-degeneracy}

A rule may be well-typed and still say nothing. Before a rule is admitted we
require, over $\mathcal{D}_\Sigma$:
\[
\begin{array}{ll}
  \mathrm{sat}\bigl(\llbracket\varphi_{\mathrm{when}}\rrbracket\bigr)
    & \textsc{[unsatisfiable\_antecedent]} \\
  \mathrm{sat}\bigl(\neg\Phi_R\bigr)
    & \textsc{[vacuous\_rule]} \\
  \mathrm{sat}\bigl(\llbracket\varphi_{\mathrm{when}}\rrbracket \wedge
                    \llbracket\varphi_{\mathrm{then}}\rrbracket\bigr)
    & \textsc{[unsatisfiable\_obligation]}
\end{array}
\]
and warn when $\neg\llbracket\varphi_{\mathrm{when}}\rrbracket$ is unsatisfiable
(the rule applies to every patient). The second condition is the important one:
a rule no patient can violate does not fail loudly --- it reports
\textsc{certified-compliant} for everyone, which is indistinguishable from a
rule that works. Duplicate names denoting one concept are merged before these
checks, since two symbols for one concept can make a tautology satisfiable.

\subsection{Fragments}
\label{sec:fragments}

$\mathrm{frag}(\varphi)$ returns the weakest of $\mathrm{PROP} <
\mathrm{LIA} < \mathrm{MTL}$ containing $\varphi$, computed from the syntax
tree: boolean and code atoms are $\mathrm{PROP}$; any ordered numeric
comparison, including a $\mathsf{Delta}$ ratio, is $\mathrm{LIA}$; any
$\mathsf{Since}$ is $\mathrm{MTL}$. Reported per statement, this makes the
logical strength each recommendation required a property of the output rather
than of an annotator.

\subsection{What is and is not guaranteed}

The validator enforces, for every emitted rule: well-formedness over $\Sigma$;
non-degeneracy; the presence of at least one action atom in the consequent; and
that every name occurring in the rule carries either a version-pinned
terminology binding or an explicit unbound declaration.

We do \emph{not} claim \emph{faithfulness} --- that $\Phi_R$ means what the
source sentence means. That is a question about natural language and is not
decidable by the tooling; it is why every rule is delivered beside the sentence
it was derived from, and why statements that cannot be encoded are returned as
first-class refusals carrying a reason, rather than approximated.
